Getting support starts with one small step.

You don’t need a diagnosis or a crisis to reach out. This page helps you pick someone to contact and gives you words to start the conversation.

Choose who to contact

Pick whichever feels easiest. There’s no wrong choice, and you can try another one later.

Someone at school

For when you’d like to talk in person with someone at school, such as a guidance counsellor or student support staff.

How to start
  1. Ask the school office: “How do I talk to guidance or student support?”
  2. Or tell a teacher you trust that you’d like to see a counsellor.
  3. Use the starter message for school staff, out loud or in a message.
What happens next
Every school does this differently. You might get an appointment time, or be asked to come back later. It’s okay to ask how it works.
Privacy
Privacy rules vary by school. If it matters to you, ask “Who else would find out about what I share?” before going into detail.

A Canadian youth support service

For when you’d rather talk to someone outside your school or family, at any time of day. Kids Help Phone offers free support to young people in Canada, 24/7, in English and French.

How to start
  • Call 1-800-668-6868 Talk with a professional counsellor. Other languages are available through interpreters.
  • Text CONNECT to 686868 Text with a volunteer crisis responder.
What happens next

Calling: you’ll hear a welcome message and a few menu options, then a counsellor greets you. You may wait in a queue first.

Texting: the first few replies are automated messages about the service and privacy. Then a volunteer joins. Text STOP to end at any time.

Privacy
Kids Help Phone describes its conversations as confidential. Texting is confidential but not anonymous. If they believe you or someone else is at risk of serious harm, they may need to share information, for example with emergency services.
Source
Kids Help Phone official website

A trusted adult

For when there’s an adult in your life you feel safe around, like a parent or caregiver, teacher, coach, relative, or family friend.

How to start
  1. Pick one person you feel comfortable with.
  2. Find a calm moment, or send a message asking to talk.
  3. Use the starter message for a trusted adult.
What happens next
People react in different ways, and some need a moment to take it in. If the first person isn’t helpful, that’s not your fault. You can try someone else or another option here.
Privacy
An adult may want to involve others to help keep you safe. You can ask, “Can we talk about who else needs to know?”

Not sure what to say?

Use these as they are, change them, or read them out loud. Nothing is sent unless you send it.

For school staff

I’ve been having a hard time and would like to talk to someone. How can I get started?

For a youth support service

Hi, this is my first time reaching out. I’ve been having a hard time and I’m not sure where to start.

For a trusted adult

I don’t know exactly how to explain it, but I could use some support. Can we talk?

Make a small plan optional

Deciding who and when ahead of time can make the first step easier.

If I decide I need support, I will contact person or service by day/time.

e.g. my school counsellor, Kids Help Phone, my aunt

Need help right now?

Emergency or in danger
If your safety is at risk, or you need police, firefighters or an ambulance, call 9-1-1 or go to your nearest emergency department.
Thinking about suicide
Call 9-8-8 or text 9-8-8, 24/7, anywhere in Canada, in English or French. This is also for you if you’re worried about someone else, or not sure whether what you’re feeling counts.
Talk to someone today
Kids Help Phone is available 24/7: call 1-800-668-6868 or text CONNECT to 686868.
